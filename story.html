The Legend of Villa Anežka

It is said that in 1913, Anežka Sokołowska and her husband, Viktor König, decided to build a house among the rolling hills and hop fields of the Žatec region.

Viktor was a prosperous hop merchant, but for Anežka, the house was meant to be more than a country residence. She was a woman of the arts, with a deep love of music, painting, literature and architecture. She dreamed of creating a place where one could escape Prague — to work, to think, to talk, and simply to be together.

To bring the house to life, they commissioned the young Prague architect Karel Vojtěch, known for combining the elegance of Art Nouveau with the emerging spirit of the new century. Together, they envisioned a villa closely connected to its surroundings: its generous windows opening towards the hop fields, forests and distant hills.

Before long, Villa Anežka became a meeting place for artists, musicians, writers and actors. During the summer months, guests arrived from Prague — some for a few days, others for several weeks. There were musical evenings, conversations about art, passionate debates about the changing times, and long walks along the country roads.

The villa also carries an old story.

It is said that Bedřich Smetana once stopped here and stayed longer than he had intended. There is no way to prove the story today. Yet the house still has a chair by the library window which, according to local tradition, was once chosen by the celebrated composer himself.

Whether it is true or not, no one can say.

But perhaps this is how the memory of a house is made.

Some things are written into history. Others are simply passed down from one generation to the next.
